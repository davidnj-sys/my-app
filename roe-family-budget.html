The plan behind the numbers

Four things we're doing on purpose. Everything above exists to make these work.

1 · Rebuild savings while the construction bills land

Savings sits at about $24,000 right now. Between the balcony/roof project ($2,800 down, three more $2,800 checks coming) and ~$2,500 for the neighbor's railing work, we'll dip to roughly $18,000 in November — then climb right back.

Starting in September, $750 moves to savings on every payday, before anything else. Not what's left over at the end of the month — first. That's the one habit that makes the whole thing work.

Projected savings balance, thousands. The dip is the construction; the climb is the rebuild.

2 · Debt-free by January 2028

Everything we're financing has an end date, and none of it charges interest as long as we stay on schedule. The one that matters most is Best Buy — it's "no interest" only if it's fully paid before its deadlines, so it gets a fixed $250/month on autopay and we don't put anything new on that card.

Gold cap = final payment. After each one ends, that money is re-aimed, not re-spent.

3 · College savings that grows by itself

The 529 goes to $200/month in September. Then every time a financed item above finishes, its payment rolls into the 529 automatically. With the next two raises adding $200 each, we go from $200 to $778/month by fall 2028 — without ever feeling a new expense, because the money was already leaving our account.

$200 → $280 (Apr '27) → $378 (Jun '27) → $578 (Sept '27) → $778 (Sept '28), per month.

4 · The cruise pays for itself

The Mediterranean cruise ($8,900 due March 2027, plus ~$4,000 airfare) is not coming out of savings or the monthly budget. It's mapped to its own money: your summer work, Dave's mentoring income, Robinhood rewards, Amex points, vacation-day sellback, and one of the health-stipend installments. The rule that makes it work: when your summer checks arrive, they go straight into savings and get fenced off — not parked in checking where they evaporate.

What's left is genuinely ours

After savings, bills, debt, the kids, and groceries all leave first, there's about $5,700 a month that isn't assigned to anything. That covers the irregular stuff (vet, repairs, gifts, copays) with plenty left for actual fun — dinners, games, trips. No category tracking, no guilt. The only daily habit: glance at the savings balance on payday. If it's climbing, everything is working.

Where these numbers come from: our Amex and Robinhood statements (March–June 2026), the Verizon and Best Buy bills, the Progressive quote, and both pay stubs. Nothing here is estimated from memory.
